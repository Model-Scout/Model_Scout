## Public mathematical mapping

The public article represents an observation through

\[
y=\mathcal{O}(x\mid c)+\varepsilon,
\]

where:

- \(x\) is an unobserved or incompletely observed state;
- \(\mathcal{O}\) is the observation process;
- \(c\) is the declared observational context;
- \(y\) is the resulting observation or conditionally admissible output;
- \(\varepsilon\) represents unresolved variation or error.

The context \(c\) includes provenance, protocol, units, uncertainty, effective resolution, sampling bias, and missingness.

Under this formulation, an observed value is not treated as self-interpreting. Its admissible meaning depends on how it was produced and on which additional observations are mathematically and procedurally compatible with it.

This enables the framework to:

- compare heterogeneous records without declaring them identical;
- preserve multiple compatible explanations when the evidence is insufficient;
- narrow the compatible-state region when higher-resolution evidence becomes available;
- restrict a requested inference when its required evidence is absent;
- separate a successfully calculated value from permission to assign it a physical meaning.

The published equation is a public abstraction. Implementation-specific converter functions, thresholds, calibration rules, source mappings, and internal decision logic are not disclosed.
