## 🚀 30-Second Preview

```
You: We're a $50k MRR Chinese B2B SaaS. Plan US market entry in 90 days.
Agent (with gingiris-go-global):

  Phase 0 — Validation (Day 1-15): is the move worth it?
    Test signals to gather:
    [ ] 15 cold US prospects: do they have the problem you solve?
    [ ] 5 reverse-translation tests: does your value prop survive translation?
    [ ] Pricing reality check: USD pricing vs USD CAC ratio
    [ ] Legal pre-check: data residency, payment methods, contract law

    Kill criteria: if <30% of prospects validate problem urgency → DO NOT expand
    Go criteria: ≥50% problem validation + viable CAC → proceed

  Phase 1 — Positioning recalibration (Day 16-30):
    • Rewrite homepage from "company first" → "use case first" (US prefers utility)
    • Translate (NOT localize): pricing, legal, support hours, payment options
    • Customer case studies: get 3 US logos before launch
    • Update Schema.org JSON-LD with US market signals

  Phase 2 — First-100 US users (Day 31-60):
    Channels ranked by ROI for B2B SaaS US entry:
    1. Product Hunt (US founders, free tier hunt) — see gingiris-launch
    2. Hacker News Show HN — see gingiris-opensource
    3. dev.to + Hashnode long-form (SEO + audience)
    4. LinkedIn Sales Navigator outbound (high-touch, paid)
    5. SEO English content (slow, compounds — see gingiris-seo-geo)

  Phase 3 — Monetization (Day 61-90):
    Pricing strategy: 2x Chinese USD pricing (US pays for trust/quality signals)
    Payment: Stripe + PayPal mandatory; ACH for >$500/mo plans
    Contracts: standard SaaS MSA (lawyer-reviewed) — NOT Chinese contract translated
```

Reverse-validate before expand, then play to local norms — not "translate everything."

## 🆕 What's New (July 2026): Conversion Gates, the 80/20 Localization Formula, and a Fundraising Playbook

This update folds a new round of firsthand founder evidence into the SOP. Five upgrades:

### 1. A conversion gate before you scale

Do not pour budget into acquisition until signup-to-paid conversion clears the bar: **ToC AI products >= 1.5%** (good products reach 6-8%), **ToB 10-15%+**. Of the 100-200+ teams taught this framework, fewer than 10 fully executed it — and those teams reached 4-8%+ conversion and raised their next round more smoothly. Proof case: one team held off on scaling for six quiet months, then hit ~$5,000 MRR on ~10K signups once the funnel converted.

### 2. Going global is subtraction, not addition

The working formula is **80% standardized SOP + 20% local adaptation**. The 20% is not translation — it means:

- **Integrate the local ecosystem** — plug into the tools, marketplaces, and communities users already live in
- **Align with local holidays** — campaign calendars follow the market's rhythm, not headquarters'
- **Adapt to culture** — messaging, support tone, and pricing psychology all localize

Also calibrate your assumptions *down*: digital maturity in Southeast Asia — and even in the US/EU — is often lower than teams expect. A guaranteed 24-hour response time is itself a competitive weapon.

### 3. Charge from day 0

Even a $1 price plants the expectation that the product is not free — and paying users give you higher-quality feedback than free users ever will. Delaying monetization does not "protect growth"; it trains your funnel to attract the wrong users.

### 4. The org ladder: revenue before headcount 8

Team-scaling checkpoints observed across the interview set:

| Headcount | What changes |
|:---|:---|
| ~3 people | One decision maker; everyone ships |
| 6-8 people | Full product-eng squad forms |
| 10-12 people | A leader layer becomes necessary |

Rule of thumb: get to revenue before you hit headcount 8. Lean benchmarks recalled in the interviews: Notion ran on roughly 4-8 people at million-user scale; ClickUp reached $1M ARR with about 24 people.

### 5. Fundraising, firsthand

Two data points from the same founder journey: the angel round took **~400 investor meetings over ~12 months**, while the **$8M Pre-A locked in about one month** after open-source traction landed (founder-reported; ~$10M total across 3 rounds). Traction compresses fundraising timelines more than any deck edit. The update also adds a narrative pivot framework — how to reframe the same company for different investor theses — plus one tactical rule: practice your pitch on low-priority investors first. The skill now ships a full fundraising playbook reference (`references/fundraising-playbook.md`).
