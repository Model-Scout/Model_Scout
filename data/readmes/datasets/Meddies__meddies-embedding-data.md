# Meddies Embedding Data

Query/passage pairs for training multilingual retrieval embeddings, generated by the Meddies
Research synthetic pipeline. The viewer exposes `dataset_derived`, `synthetic_monolingual`,
`synthetic_cross_lingual` and `synthetic_language_unverified` as separate configurations. Notes below describe the synthetic cross-lingual subset.

## Synthetic cross-lingual

Each row is one generated query paired with the source passage it was grounded in. Queries are
produced by an LLM reading a passage split into numbered evidence blocks, under a program-owned
plan that fixes the information need, query shape, register, lexical relation and target
language per slot. The model never chooses those axes; it writes the query.

**Prompt contract:** `synthetic_v3-calibration-retrievable-query-v7`.

### Cross-lingual by design

The query language is drawn per request independently of the passage language, so a Korean
passage yields Vietnamese, English or Chinese queries as often as Korean ones. Roughly two
thirds of rows are cross-lingual. `document_language` and `query_language` are stored
separately, and `requested_query_language` records what the planner asked for before
validation confirmed what was produced.

### Domains

| domain | source material |
|---|---|
| `general` | Wikipedia across 17 languages, via `Meddies/wiki` |
| `medical` | clinical practice guidelines and medical reference text |
| `legal` | statutes, regulations and codified law across 7 languages |

### Grounding and validation

Every query is generated against a passage carrying at least ten evidence blocks, so a passage
cannot be asked for ten near-identical questions. Queries pass deterministic validation before
promotion: fastText language identification against the requested language, a token-length
policy calibrated per language, a retrievability gate rejecting vague subjects and
meta-references to the passage, and exact-match duplicate rejection against both the current run
and previously published data.

### Provenance

Every row carries its source dataset and pinned upstream revision, the source admission record
that authorised generation, the prompt contract version, the tokenizer identity, the transform
digest, and the execution identity of the run that produced it. `cohort` names the operating-day
run a row came from.

### Personal data

Source corpora are screened before admission and again by an independent audit that does not
share the admission screen's patterns. Judicial corpora that name private individuals — parties,
claimants, litigants — are excluded rather than filtered, after rule-based redaction was
measured and found unreliable on them. Rows identified as carrying an identifiable private
person are removed from the reservoir, not from the output.

### Licensing

The general subset derives from Wikipedia under
[CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) and this derived bundle carries
the same licence. Medical and legal subsets derive from sources under varying terms, including
some that permit non-commercial use only; Meddies Research is a non-commercial research effort.
Consult the per-row `source_admission_license` and `source_admission_attribution` fields before
redistributing any subset, because the top-level licence field cannot express a mixed corpus.

### Known limitations

- Duplicate rejection is exact-match on a normalised query key. Cosmetic rewrites of the same
  information need survive it; near-duplicate rate is materially higher than the exact rate.
- A share of rows carry a null `query_language` where language identification was confident
  enough to promote but not to label. Filtering on language drops them.
- Hard negatives are not included. Train with in-batch negatives or mine them separately.

> **Default configuration:** `synthetic_cross_lingual` is the sole default. `synthetic_language_unverified` is opt-in quarantine data; do not train on it without review.
