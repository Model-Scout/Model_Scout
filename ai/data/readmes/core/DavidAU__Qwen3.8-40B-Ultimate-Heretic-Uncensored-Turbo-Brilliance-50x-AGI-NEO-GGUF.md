<H2>SETTINGS / QUANTS:</H2>

---

<b>Model Suggested Settings [from testing]:</b>
- Temp .5 to 1 
- Rep pen 1 [off]
- TopK 64, min p: 0.05, topp: 0.95 (tester settings)
- Min context window of 24,000 tokens [24k] is strongly suggested or higher (model max is 256k/266,000 tokens)
- Suggest 64k+ context windows for larger/more complex use cases/prompts/multi-turn conversations.
- NO caching (suggested)

<B>TOOL CALLING:</B>
- temp .6 to .7 ; suggest using "medium" / "xhigh" reasoning mode to start.
- quants MIN: Q6, Q8 ; you maybe able to get away with IQ4xs/Q4km in some cases.
- use of (Agent) "007" (AND AGI versions) should be carefully tested, and note this may come with a reduction in speed in some cases due to the "007" AI Construct size.

<B>Critical - Quants / Convo:</B>
- The quant you use will directly impact the new reasoning modes -> Higher quant -> Better performance.
- "MAX" quants (MTP/non MTP) will give the best performance; as they have the output tensor at BF16 (full precision) and MTP tensors at Q8.
- MTP/NON mtp quants without MAX will give you standard - but very strong - performance.
- If you want to have multiple turn convos -> set the context at maximum 256k.
- Reasoning modes and how to access / use are listed below.

Standard quants are NEO IMATRIX MTP.

<B>Benchmarking:</B>
- The default reasoning mode is "xhigh", just like regular Qwen 3.8 27B models.
- If you want to bench mark other modes from Turbo Brilliance make sure the mode is MATCHED with the test(s) otherwise the results will be lower.
- Reasoning/instruct modes, AI and AGI constructs are designed to boost direct and outlier performance for SPECIFIC use cases (see examples below).
- Instruct modes will generally perform at a lower level than reasoning/thinking modes except for some metric types.
- Make sure you set your context to at least 32k (especially for "xhigh", "x" modes and AGI modes) for benchmarking and your benchmarking systems accept/allow this otherwise the "right answer" might be "cut off" so to speak.

# CONCLUSION

Australia's unique position with vast clear-sky regions, extensive desert areas, and growing cooling demands makes it an ideal nation to pioneer large-scale radiative cooling technologies. The comprehensive framework presented in this document demonstrates that with strategic planning, adequate investment, and supportive policy, Australia can transform its nighttime radiative cooling potential into a major contributor to global temperature reduction while simultaneously addressing domestic energy, water, and agricultural needs.

The ten strategies outlined represent complementary approaches that, when integrated effectively, can deliver:
- **21,410 TWh/year** of cooling capacity
- **23,550 Mt/year** of CO₂ reduction
- **$6.4 billion AUD/year** in revenue
- **200,000 peak jobs** during construction
- **100,000 steady-state jobs** during operation

The journey requires commitment at all levels—national, state, and local—to fully realize Australia's potential as a global leader in radiative cooling innovation and climate action.
