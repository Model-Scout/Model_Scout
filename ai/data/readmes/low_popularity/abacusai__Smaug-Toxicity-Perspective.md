## 1. Model Introduction

Smaug-Toxicity-Perspective is an open, multilingual toxicity classifier from Abacus.AI. It returns the seven attributes of Google's Perspective API (TOXICITY, SEVERE_TOXICITY, IDENTITY_ATTACK, INSULT, PROFANITY, THREAT, SEXUALLY_EXPLICIT) as calibrated probabilities, and it ships with a server that answers Perspective API requests in the same JSON format, so teams moving off the Perspective API before Google shuts it down on December 31, 2026 can keep their code and change one URL.

- **More accurate across languages.** On Horizon-Labs' own published benchmark (their evaluation code, their rows), it ranks toxic comments in 15 languages better than the general-purpose open classifiers from Horizon-Labs and Detoxify: TextDetox ROC AUC 89.0, against 85.4 for Horizon-Labs multilingual-toxicity-base and 67.8 for Detoxify multilingual. It is also more accurate at the usual 0.5 threshold (F1 65.2 vs 43.3).
- **Fewer false alarms than LLM guards.** It ranks toxic comments better than Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B, a safety LLM 27 times its size (AUC 88.4 vs 84.6), and flags 8.1% of clean comments against 38.8%. At the 0.5 threshold Qwen3Guard catches more of the toxic ones (88% against 59%) because it flags so much more.
- **Closest to the Perspective API.** Trained in part on the Perspective API's own published scores, it orders texts the way Perspective does more closely than any open model we tested, in English and in 17 languages (section 3). Swapped in at Perspective's 0.8 threshold (the default in Coral's documentation), it flags 95% of what Perspective flags in English text it never trained on, against 18% for Horizon-Labs base, and 85% on multilingual web text. It scores toxic text more decisively than Perspective, so at 0.8 it flags 1.2 times as many English texts and 1.9 times as many multilingual ones; section 6 gives the threshold that keeps your flag volume.
- **Runs anywhere, stays reproducible.** 22 ms per comment on 4 CPU threads with the 8-bit ONNX export, which agrees with float32 on 99.9% of decisions at 0.5; a CPU Docker image; about 2,200 comments per second on one H100; Apache 2.0, and every score is reproducible by pinning a commit.

This card describes the model, its evaluation against the Perspective API's own scores and the open alternatives, how to move from the Perspective API, and the known limitations. Training data are listed in section 7; training rows are not released.

## 4. Training Approach

**What the scores mean.** Each output is the probability that a typical rater would apply the label, following the Perspective API's attribute definitions and the Civil Comments annotation guidelines:

- TOXICITY: rude, disrespectful or unreasonable language likely to make someone leave a discussion.
- SEVERE_TOXICITY: very hateful, aggressive or disrespectful language; much less sensitive to mild profanity than TOXICITY.
- IDENTITY_ATTACK: negative or hateful language aimed at someone because of an identity such as religion, ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexual orientation or disability.
- INSULT: insulting, inflammatory or demeaning language towards a person or group.
- PROFANITY: swear words, curse words and other obscene language.
- THREAT: an intention to inflict pain, injury or violence on a person or group.
- SEXUALLY_EXPLICIT: references to sexual acts, sexual body parts or other lewd content.

**Method.** Seven sigmoid outputs trained with binary cross-entropy on the fraction of human raters who chose each label, so a comment half the raters found toxic targets 0.5. Attributes a dataset does not label are left out of the loss rather than set to 0. Non-toxic comments that mention an identity group count double, to reduce false flags on identity mentions. Two epochs, maximum length 256 tokens, learning rate 5e-5, batch 64. Each target is the mean of the label and a teacher's score (an XLM-R-large model with 560M parameters, trained on the same data, the Perspective-scored texts below also counted twice); on 374,000 unlabelled native web segments in Hebrew, Hinglish, Tatar and Ukrainian (FineWeb-2, HPLT 2.0) the teacher's scores alone are the targets.

**The Perspective API's own scores.** About 530,000 training texts carry the Perspective API's published scores instead of rater fractions: web text in 17 languages from PolygloToxicityPrompts (ptp-full without the evaluation texts; scored in 2024) and three quarters of the RealToxicityPrompts prompts with their continuations (scored in 2020). They count twice in the loss. On German and Portuguese pages the target is the mean of Perspective's score and an earlier version's, because Perspective gives many harmless pages there 0.5 to 0.75 (section 5); PolygloToxicityPrompts has no SEXUALLY_EXPLICIT score, so that target comes from the earlier version too.

**Calibration.** One temperature per attribute, fitted on held-out Civil Comments rater fractions (TOXICITY T = 0.885) and folded into the final layer, so the published weights need no post-processing.

**Versions.** Scores change only when you change the model. Pin a commit of this repository (`revision=` in transformers, `MODEL_REVISION` for the server) and every score stays reproducible; later releases will go out as new commits with their evaluation in this card.

## 5. Known Behaviors and Limitations

- **High thresholds flag more texts than Perspective.** This model scores toxic text more decisively than Perspective does: at 0.9 it flags 1.8 times as many held-out English texts as Perspective and 2.9 times as many multilingual web texts, whose 2024 Perspective scores rarely exceed 0.9. If you act automatically at 0.8 or 0.9, start from the volume-matched threshold in section 6, or measure it on your own traffic with `compare_with_perspective.py`.
- **German and Portuguese web pages: Perspective is only partly reproduced, on purpose.** Perspective gives many harmless German and Portuguese web pages 0.5 to 0.75 in PolygloToxicityPrompts (a health-forum question about a pill, a parenting forum, a paintbrush product page). Training on those scores alone copied the habit, so for these two languages the training target blends Perspective's score with an earlier version's. This model flags 47.5% (German) and 50.0% (Portuguese) of Perspective's flags there; in blind reads of the German and Portuguese pages that it flags and the earlier version does not, 37 to 57 of every 100 were harmless pages that Perspective also flags. If a false flag on a German or Portuguese web page costs you more than a missed one, use a higher threshold for them.
- **Long texts.** The model reads 256 tokens at a time. The bundled server scores longer texts in overlapping windows and reports the highest window score per attribute; the transformers pipeline with `truncation=True` scores only the first 256 tokens.
- **Only the seven production attributes.** Perspective's experimental attributes (such as FLIRTATION) and its bridging attributes (such as REASONING or CURIOSITY) are not provided, and there is no language detection.
- **Automated removal.** The AfriHate authors ask that models built with their data not be used for automated removal. We pass that request on: use the scores to prioritise human review, not to remove content without a person in the loop.
- Like every toxicity model, it can over-flag identity mentions and reclaimed or quoted language and miss sarcasm or implicit abuse; labels are subjective and culture-dependent. Do not use it as the only basis for decisions about people.

## 6. Deployment

### Quickstart

Try it in the browser first: the [demo Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/abacusai/Smaug-Toxicity-Perspective) scores text in any language, answers requests in the Perspective API's format and works as an MCP tool for agents.

```python
from transformers import pipeline

clf = pipeline("text-classification", model="abacusai/Smaug-Toxicity-Perspective", top_k=None)
print(clf("You are a complete idiot and nobody wants you here.", truncation=True))
# [[{'label': 'TOXICITY', 'score': 0.95}, {'label': 'INSULT', 'score': 0.95}, ...]]
```

The config marks the task as multi-label, so the pipeline applies a sigmoid per attribute; `truncation=True` scores the first 256 tokens. Tested with transformers 4.57 and 5.14.

**4. What stays the same, and what differs.**

- Same: `POST /v1alpha1/comments:analyze` with `comment.text`, `requestedAttributes` (with `scoreThreshold`), `spanAnnotations` (per-sentence `spanScores`), `languages`, `clientToken` and `doNotStore`; the seven attribute names; scores as `{"value": p, "type": "PROBABILITY"}`; the 20,480-byte limit; Google-style error bodies; `comments:suggestscore` (accepted, and stored only if you set `FEEDBACK_FILE`); and the discovery document that Google's client libraries read.
- Different: other attributes (experimental, bridging) return an error; there is no language detection, so any language is accepted and `languages` echoes your request; there are no quotas or rate limits; API keys are optional (`API_KEYS=key1,key2`); scores are close to Perspective's but not identical (section 3); and your text never leaves your servers.

```yaml
litellm_settings:
  guardrails:
    - guardrail_name: smaug-toxicity-perspective
      litellm_params:
        guardrail: generic_guardrail_api
        mode: pre_call            # or post_call
        api_base: http://localhost:8080
        additional_provider_specific_params:
          threshold: 0.8          # BLOCKED when any attribute reaches it
```

- **Browser and Node.js (transformers.js).** The int8 export runs with transformers.js 3 (tested with 3.8.1 on Node.js):

```js
import { pipeline } from "@huggingface/transformers";

const clf = await pipeline("text-classification", "abacusai/Smaug-Toxicity-Perspective", { dtype: "int8" });
console.log(await clf("You are a complete idiot and nobody wants you here.", { top_k: 7 }));
```

transformers.js runs `onnx/model_int8.onnx` (a 309 MB download; `onnx/model_quantized.onnx` is the same file under the name transformers.js looks for by default in the browser, so the call also works there without `dtype`), plain dynamic int8 (its bundled ONNX Runtime 1.21 cannot load the 8-bit file the server uses), so its scores are close to the server's but not identical: it moves scores by 0.014 on average (99th percentile 0.09) and flips 1.4% of decisions at 0.5, and it tokenizes runs of two or more spaces differently (float32 scores up to 0.02 apart on such texts). Use the server or Python where exact scores matter.

- **Text Embeddings Inference (TEI).** Hugging Face's serving container runs the float32 ONNX export, but its `/predict` route applies a softmax across labels by default, which is wrong for seven independent attributes (the scores would sum to 1). Ask for raw scores and apply a sigmoid yourself:

```bash
docker run -p 8080:80 ghcr.io/huggingface/text-embeddings-inference:cpu-1.9 --model-id abacusai/Smaug-Toxicity-Perspective
curl -s localhost:8080/predict -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"inputs": "You are an idiot", "raw_scores": true}'
# [{"score": 3.15, "label": "TOXICITY"}, {"score": 3.03, "label": "INSULT"}, ...]
# each score is a logit: probability = 1 / (1 + exp(-score)), so TOXICITY = 0.96
```

With `raw_scores` and the sigmoid, TEI 1.9.4 matches the transformers pipeline to 2e-07 (six texts in three languages; without `raw_scores` the same insult gets TOXICITY 0.53 instead of 0.96). `sentence_bert_config.json` in this repository limits TEI to the 256-token window the model was trained on; without it TEI accepts 8,192 tokens, and its warm-up ran out of memory in an 8 GB container. Like the pipeline, TEI scores only the first 256 tokens of a longer text, where the bundled server scores every window. The same applies to an Inference Endpoint that runs the TEI engine.

- **ONNX.** `onnx/model.onnx` is float32 and matches PyTorch to 1.8e-07. `onnx/model_int8_block32.onnx` (322 MB; 8-bit block-wise weights with int8 compute; the default of the server and the Docker image) moves scores by 0.0008 on average (maximum 0.013) and changes 0.08% of decisions at 0.5 on 2,200 multilingual texts with ONNX Runtime's standard CPU kernels. With ONNX Runtime's generic CPU kernels (x86, Graviton, Apple M1 to M3) it is about 2.1 times as fast as float32 (23 vs 49 ms per comment on 4 threads of an Apple M5); on Apple M4 or newer, float32 is at least as fast (19 ms) and exact. It needs an ONNX Runtime with 8-bit MatMulNBits (tested with 1.31). `onnx/model_int8.onnx` (309 MB) is plain dynamic int8 for transformers.js and older runtimes. It quantizes activations per tensor, so on most CPUs and in browsers it moves scores by 0.014 on average (99th percentile 0.09) and flips 1.4% of decisions at 0.5; only on Apple M4 or newer, where ONNX Runtime's KleidiAI kernels quantize per row, does it stay much closer to float32. The server runs any of them with `BACKEND=onnx` (`ONNX_FILE` picks the file).
